Who it's for

Counting a thousand books with a clipboard is a two-day job.

Every spring, someone has to find out what the school actually owns. The books come back stacked in no particular order — six copies of one algebra text, one stray copy of a title nobody remembers buying. Doing it on paper means writing titles by hand, then typing them again at a desk, then reconciling a tally that stopped being accurate an hour in.

Shelfcount is built for the person doing that count: the textbook coordinator, the school librarian, the media specialist, the aide who got handed the job. Pick up a book, scan the barcode, set it down. That's the whole motion.

How it works

Three steps, and only one of them takes any time.

  1. Name the count

    Start an inventory event — "Book Room B, Spring 2027." Everything you scan from here belongs to it.

  2. Scan

    Click the scan box and go. Scan as fast as you can pick up books; the app keeps up and never makes you wait between beeps.

  3. Review and export

    Fix anything flagged, then send the finished count out as a spreadsheet or a printable report.

No scanner yet?

The laptop's own camera counts too.

On the scan screen, Scan with camera turns the webcam into a barcode reader — hold a book up to it and it counts exactly like a scanner read, with the same beep and the same undo. It's there for trying Shelfcount out before buying anything, and for the shelf you find on the way back to your desk. A handheld scanner is still much faster for a full cart.

Camera scanning uses barcode decoding built into the browser, which today means Chrome or Edge — the button simply isn't shown in browsers that can't do it. A USB or Bluetooth scanner works in every browser, on every platform.

What it does

The tedious parts, handled.

  • Titles fill themselves in

    Scan an unfamiliar ISBN and the app looks up the title, author, publisher, and copyright year for you.

  • It only learns a book once

    Every book you've ever scanned stays in your catalog. Next year's count recognizes it instantly — no lookup, no waiting.

  • Duplicates add themselves up

    Scan the same title six times and the count reads six. No searching a list to find the row you already made.

  • You can always override it

    Wrong title, missing year, no record anywhere? Type it in. Your correction sticks, and it applies everywhere that book appears — including in counts you finished last year.

  • A list you don't have to scroll

    The event screen shows just enough — title, author, year, count — to confirm you scanned the right book at a glance.

  • Mistakes are easy to take back

    Undo the last scan, or set aside any row further back. Nothing is ever deleted, so anything set aside can be put straight back.

  • A damaged barcode isn't a dead end

    Type the number in instead. A barcode that isn't an ISBN at all still gets counted, and can be attached to a book afterwards — every scan of it, including the ones you already did.

  • Nothing gets lost

    Close the laptop mid-shelf, come back tomorrow, and pick up exactly where the count left off.

  • Compare against last year

    Line this count up against any earlier one: what's missing, what's short, worst first. Print it and walk the shelves with it.

  • Finish with a real deliverable

    Export to a spreadsheet — CSV or Excel — for whoever asked for the numbers.

The book room has no wifi

Scanning never stops just because the internet does.

Shelfcount counts every scan the instant it happens, whether or not it can reach the outside world. Book details it doesn't recognize yet go into a queue. The moment you're back on a network — down the hall, or the next morning — it quietly fills in everything it missed and tells you if anything still needs your eyes.

What you can count

It starts with textbooks. It doesn't end there.

  • Looks itself up Textbooks and classroom sets
  • Looks itself up Library books and paperbacks
  • Counted, named by you DVDs, VHS, and other media
  • Counted, named by you Tagged equipment and non-media assets

Anything with a barcode gets counted. Books carry an ISBN, so the app can look them up for you; a DVD case or an asset tag doesn't, so you name it once — and every scan of that barcode, before and after, counts toward it from then on.

What you need

A laptop and a scanner. That's the shopping list.

Your laptop
Windows or Linux. Shelfcount lives in one folder — copy that folder to back up everything, or move it to another machine.
A barcode scanner
Any inexpensive USB or Bluetooth handheld scanner works. Plug it in; there's nothing to configure. Or start with the laptop's camera and buy one later.
Your data
Stays on your laptop, in one file you can copy anywhere. No account to create, no student information involved. What leaves the laptop is a short list.
Internet
Helpful, never required. It's only ever used to look up book details — and to tell you when a new version is available, which you choose whether to install.
